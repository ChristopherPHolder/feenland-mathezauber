# Feenland Mathezauber

A touch-first math adventure for children, with responsive phone, tablet and desktop layouts. The user-approved reference is a fairyland with a red-haired fairy, green leaf dress, cream surfaces, dark blue text and pink primary actions. Keep artwork simple in the first delivery.

The Home screen offers Continue/Start, Explore Worlds, a Fairy games hub that opens the castle, the crocodile and the magic walls with one tap, cumulative achievements and a Settings gear. Launch languages are en/de/es/fr; language selection lives in Settings. Content follows World → Chapter → Level → Challenge with stable IDs. Progress stays locally and earned accomplishments survive catalog growth. All old unicorn, berry and number-bridge content is removed by explicit user request.

Current delivery includes the twenty-wave Fairy Tower Defense campaign, plus nine selectable math stages covering tens and ones, addition, subtraction and mixed practice up to 30. The game start screen offers the castle and every stage as large picture cards showing the stage number, example equation, skill and range, tapping a card starts that game right away, and every wave is open from the start so a child can tap straight into any of them. Difficulty and pace are independent remembered preferences. Real practice completion is saved separately from demos. Existing campaign saves remain valid.

The Numbers world also offers The Hungry Crocodile, a six-level comparison game built around the classroom trick that the crocodile's open mouth always eats the bigger number. Levels move from comparing numbers up to 20 and 100, to adding or subtracting before comparing, to two calculations on either side, and finally mixed numbers and sums up to 100. Each round feeds the crocodile ten snacks; a wrong pick shows comparison bars and both results as help (with a dedicated reminder when both sides are equal), every level is open from the start, stars reward a careful round, and finishing always counts.

Addition Island offers Magic walls, a four-level number wall (Rechenmauer) game in which two bricks side by side always make the brick above them. The intro shows the illustrated fairy and a tiny demo wall, every level is open from the start with stars rewarding a finished level, a plus sign marks the pair of bricks that belongs to the glowing brick, and the first miss already shows the trick with a number line so a child is never left guessing.
