# Feenland Mathezauber

A touch-first math adventure for children, with responsive phone, tablet and desktop layouts. The user-approved reference is a fairyland with a blonde, blue-eyed fairy in a pale mint leaf dress and gold slippers, cream surfaces, dark blue text and pink primary actions. Keep artwork simple in the first delivery.

The Home screen offers Continue/Start, an All games button, one card per game (castle, crocodile, magic walls, missing-number stages) with its own Play button, cumulative achievements and a Settings gear. The games hub lists every game and every level as a card that starts play with one tap; nothing is ever locked, a child may pick any game or level at any time, and progress only adds stars, best scores and a "Your next step" badge. Launch languages are en/de/es/fr; language selection lives in Settings. Content follows World → Chapter → Level → Challenge with stable IDs; a chapter is a game. Progress stays locally and earned accomplishments survive catalog growth. The old berry and number-bridge content is removed by explicit user request; the unicorn returned in November 2026 as the Unicorn Rainbow Race.

Current delivery includes the twenty-wave Fairy Tower Defense campaign, plus eleven selectable math stages covering tens and ones, addition, subtraction, mixed practice and times tables (products and missing factors) up to 30. Easy times tables stay in the ×2, ×5 and ×10 rows with products up to 20; hard ones leave out ×2. The game start screen offers the castle and every stage as large picture cards showing the stage number, example equation, skill and range, tapping a card starts that game right away, and every wave is open from the start so a child can tap straight into any of them. Difficulty and pace are independent remembered preferences. Real practice completion is saved separately from demos. Existing campaign saves remain valid.

The Numbers world also offers The Hungry Crocodile, a seven-level comparison game built around the classroom trick that the crocodile's open mouth always eats the bigger number. Levels move from comparing numbers up to 20 and 100, to adding or subtracting before comparing, to two calculations on either side, mixed numbers and sums up to 100, and finally times-table facts with products up to 30 (like 4 × 5 ○ 3 × 7), where Kiki can earn a magic top hat. Each round feeds the crocodile ten snacks; a wrong pick shows comparison bars and both results as help (with a dedicated reminder when both sides are equal), every level is open from the start, stars reward a careful round, and finishing always counts. Rounds play out in Kiki’s pond: every snack scores points, three first-try answers in a row double them and six triple them in a feeding frenzy, two golden snacks per round and the big fish at the end pay extra, and the best score per level is remembered. Two stars in a level add a hat to Kiki’s wardrobe, which she wears from then on. Hovering or focusing an answer makes Kiki turn and open her jaws toward that side before the child commits, the number tiles bob on the water and get gulped when eaten, and a frog, butterfly and lily pads live in the pond.

Addition Island offers Magic walls, a four-level number wall (Rechenmauer) game in which two bricks side by side always make the brick above them. The intro shows the illustrated fairy and a tiny demo wall, every level is open from the start with stars rewarding a finished level, a plus sign marks the pair of bricks that belongs to the glowing brick, and the first miss already shows the trick with a number line so a child is never left guessing. The scene is alive: clouds and a bird drift across the sky, the two bricks to add pulse together, a placed brick drops in with a puff of dust while the fairy dives to it, the troll grows and kicks up dust as it nears the wall and tints the sky when it is close, and gems fly up when a wall is built.

The crocodile intro offers four large illustrated pace choices: No rush (the initial default), Relaxed (30 seconds per number snack / 45 for calculations), Normal (15 / 25), and Fast (8 / 15). The selected pace, including No rush, is remembered locally across visits and levels; unavailable or invalid storage falls back safely. The pace is the speed of a greedy heron rather than a visible clock: with any pace but No rush, the heron flies in across the pond toward the snack, feeding Kiki first shoos it away with up to 20 bonus points by distance, and letting it land snatches the snack, resets the combo, counts as one mistake and moves the round on. The heron pauses in a hidden tab, a child can send it away during play, and the heron rounds keep their own best score. The lily pads and Kiki can be tapped directly to answer. All four languages include the challenge.

The Tens world also offers the Unicorn Rainbow Race, a fluency game of fifteen tracks in five cups of three, each track run in six heats, in the spirit of racing maths mini-games: Lumi the unicorn gallops to the rainbow castle without stopping. Four shuffled answers sit under the sum; a right answer gives Lumi a sprint and adds to her cruising speed so a streak snowballs, a wrong one strikes that choice out, makes her stumble and halves her pace, and three in a row is a rainbow dash worth more sparkles. Every track is run in six heats that get longer and quicker and turn from day to dusk to night: a warm-up race, a key chase, a shadow run, a big race, a night chase with a longer head start, and a cup final that must be won. A race is against three friends (Cloud the pegasus, Minty the unicorn and Peach the pony); in a chase Storm the pegasus has grabbed the castle key and a head start and must be passed before the castle; in a shadow run Lumi races her own shadow, which runs the par time for that heat until the child sets a faster best. The intro shows the six heats of the chosen track with their cups and best times, a track opens on its first unfinished heat, the finish offers the next heat straight away, and race cards show how many of the six heats are done. The Meadow Cup covers adding to 10, taking away within 10 and plus and minus to 20; the Sky Cup doubles and near doubles, plus and minus to 100 and two-digit sums without carrying; the Starlight Cup the 2, 5 and 10 times tables, the 3 and 4 times tables and missing factors; the Castle Cup missing numbers, the hard times tables up to 30 and a mixed final that only a win masters; and the Rainbow Cup mixes everything in a chase, a shadow race and a Grand Prix final that must also be won. Each cup has its own scenery (a flower meadow, a path above the clouds, a starry night, a sunset with lanterns and a pink sky with rainbows arching over the track) and page colours. Finishing all three races of a cup earns a keepsake Lumi can wear in every race and in the intro line-up (a flower wreath, cloud wings, a star tiara, the castle crown and a rainbow cape), chosen in a Dress Lumi panel; a new keepsake is put on straight away. During a race the streak chip shows three pips filling toward the rainbow dash, the last fifth of the track calls the final stretch, the camera widens with speed, and a win sets off fireworks over the castle. The scene reacts to every answer: a right one bursts sparkles and speed lines around Lumi, a wrong one drops her head, kicks up dust, spins dizzy stars over her and shakes the camera; the ponies paw the ground before the start, flap their wings, swish their tails and nod as they run, and the winner rears up on the line. Race cards show the stars earned, the cup and the best time. The intro offers three remembered rival paces (Relaxed, Normal and Fast) that set how fast the others run; the shadow ignores the pace. Tracks are tuned so a race takes about a minute to a minute and a half of steady answering (fourteen to twenty questions), with later cups a little longer, and at Normal pace a child who knows the facts wins about half the time. Finishing always counts; first place earns three stars and a gold cup, a close second (within a few seconds of Storm or within a quarter of the shadow's time) two stars and silver, and every other finish one star and bronze. Best times, cups and sparkles stay on the device, the intro shows how many of the four cups are complete, the finish shows confetti, the stars and a podium line with Next race, Race again, Choose a race, the celebration and All games, and all four languages include every race. A wrong answer never reveals the result: the child keeps working the same sum out.
